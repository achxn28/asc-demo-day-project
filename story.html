The Story Behind the Numbers

Los Angeles is often seen through its public image: entertainment, tourism, and cultural production. Bridge LA pairs that story with current public data and Danielle’s story, so the numbers stay connected to actual lives.

Sheltered and unsheltered count

This chart uses the City of Los Angeles totals from the 2026 Greater Los Angeles Homeless Count and calculates the sheltered estimate from total minus unsheltered.

What this means: the chart separates people counted indoors from people counted without shelter, which helps explain why resource visibility and outreach matter.
Source: LAHSA 2026 Greater Los Angeles Homeless Count

Why visibility matters

A large unsheltered share means people and service providers need information that is accurate, current, and explicit about uncertainty. Bridge LA avoids fake availability claims and points users to provider contact information where real-time data is not public.

What this means: Danielle’s story shows why a useful tool should help people reach real providers without pretending availability is live.

What to look for in the numbers

Scale

Use the total count to understand the size of the crisis, then avoid treating that number as a complete description of people’s lives.

Exposure

The unsheltered share points to people living without stable indoor protection, which changes urgency, outreach needs, and health risk.

Limits

Counts are estimates at a point in time. Bridge LA should always show source, year, and what the metric does not measure.

Data with a face

Danielle is the reminder built into the charts.

Danielle’s path included grief, homelessness, incarceration, recovery, and reconnection with family. On Bridge LA, her story sits beside the charts to make the point clear: public data can explain scale, but it cannot contain a whole life.

That is why the Data page also checks what the site cannot prove: live beds, open appointments, and exact outcomes from a demo donation.
